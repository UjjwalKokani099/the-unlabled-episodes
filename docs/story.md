# UNLABELED — Story Bible

Nov 1, 2026 · @ujjwal_kokani

## Premise

You buy a box of unmarked VHS tapes at a Sunday flea market. Each tape is one stranger's
worst night, and somewhere in every tape stands a man holding a camcorder. By the last
tape, you understand why the box was cheap: everyone who watches the tapes becomes the
next one.

**Hook:** every chapter feels like an ordinary night job, until you notice the red
recording light in the dark.

**Setting:** an Indian city and its highways, across 1998 to 2009 for the tapes and the
present day for the hub. The everyday Indian setting (housing societies, toll plazas,
highway lodges, delivery riders) is what makes the game feel fresh.

## The Rules

The game never explains these out loud. Players piece them together across the tapes.

- **Whoever watches the tapes gets recorded.** Each tape's subject once bought the same box.
- **The box always returns to the market.** After each owner's night ends, the box goes back
  on a stall with one more tape inside.
- **The tapes are never labeled.** A label is a name, and the Cameraman takes your name
  along with your night.
- **The narration is impossible.** Each tape opens with the subject calmly narrating their
  night in past tense, as if they survived to tell it. None of them did. Nobody knows who
  recorded these voiceovers.

## The Cameraman

A thin man in a grey sweater, always holding an old shoulder camcorder over his face. He
never speaks, never runs, and never touches anyone on camera. His tell is the red REC
light on his camcorder: a small red dot in the dark. Players learn to fear any red light.

He is never explained. Is he a ghost, a person, or the tapes themselves? The game never
answers.

## The Hub

**You:** Nikhil, 24, recently moved into a rented single room in a new city. He works from
home and collects old media as a hobby. He's lonely enough that a box of mystery tapes
feels like a good Sunday.

**The room:** a bed, a desk with a laptop, a small window facing the lane, a CRT TV and a
second-hand VCR on a steel trunk. Between tapes, Nikhil does small evening chores: eats
dinner, replies to his mother's texts, charges his phone.

### How the room changes after each tape

| After  | What changes |
|--------|--------------|
| Tape 1 | A handwritten entry appears in Nikhil's own diary: today's date and "3:12 AM, out." It isn't his handwriting. |
| Tape 2 | A white van idles in the lane below his window, headlights on. It's gone if he looks away. |
| Tape 3 | A room number, 108, is scratched into the inside of his door. His room has no number. |
| Tape 4 | The doorbell rings. A delivery bag sits outside with food he never ordered, his name and address on the bill. |
| Final  | The box contains one more tape than he bought. |

## The Tapes

Each tape is about 30 to 45 minutes. Each opens with a date and time burned into the
corner, and the subject's calm voiceover.

### Tape 1: The Watchman

Raghu, 52 · Shanti Vihar Housing Society · 14 March 1998, 10:40 PM

Raghu is the night guard at a quiet four-building society. It's his first week on nights
after the old guard quit without notice.

- **Tasks:** fill the gate register, check the CCTV monitor, do patrol rounds, lock the
  terrace doors, answer the intercom.
- **The wrong thing:** at 3:12 AM, Mr. Deshmukh from flat 4B walks out of the gate without
  a word. He never comes back in. Yet every time Raghu checks the CCTV, Deshmukh is
  standing on his balcony, watching the gate.
- **Escalation:** the register has entries in handwriting that isn't Raghu's, logging his
  own movements. The intercom from 4B rings, but there's only breathing.
- **Ending:** Raghu learns from the society secretary on the phone that 4B has been empty
  for eight months. The power cuts out. Raghu goes to the meter room with a torch. On the
  CCTV monitor, the last frame shows a red dot behind him. Static.

### Tape 2: The Toll Booth

Salim, 34 · Highway toll plaza · 2 November 2001, 1:05 AM

Salim works the quiet overnight booth on an empty highway.

- **Tasks:** take payment, issue receipts, log number plates, raise and lower the barrier,
  radio the supervisor.
- **The wrong thing:** a white Omni van with the same number plate passes every 20
  minutes. The driver looks slightly different each time: older, then younger, then
  wearing Salim's own uniform.
- **Escalation:** each pass, there's one more passenger in the back. On the fourth pass,
  one of them is Raghu in his watchman's uniform, staring straight ahead.
- **Ending:** the van stops at the booth and doesn't move. The driver hands Salim a printed
  receipt with Salim's name on it. The booth lights die. The red dot glows in the van's
  back seat.

### Tape 3: The Night Desk

Meera, 27 · Sagar Lodge, a highway lodge · 21 July 2005, 11:30 PM

Meera covers the night reception at a run-down two-floor lodge with 107 rooms.

- **Tasks:** check guests in, update the ledger, take room-service calls, hand out keys,
  walk the corridors.
- **The wrong thing:** a soft-spoken guest asks for room 108. Meera tells him there isn't
  one. He smiles, takes no key and walks upstairs.
- **Escalation:** the switchboard lights up with calls from room 108. The upstairs corridor
  is longer each time she walks it.
- **Ending:** she finds room 108 at the end of the corridor. Inside is her own rented room,
  with a cardboard box of unlabeled tapes she bought weeks ago and forgot about. The TV is
  on, playing this tape. She sees herself standing in the doorway, and someone filming her
  from behind.

### Tape 4: The Last Delivery

Imran, 22 · Food delivery rider · 9 January 2009, 12:50 AM

Imran is chasing one last order to hit his daily target.

- **Tasks:** pick up the order, follow the phone's directions, ride through the city, find
  the address, call the customer.
- **The wrong thing:** the customer's name is blank and the drop pin sits on an old
  shut-down cinema, Paradise Talkies. The customer keeps texting "come up."
- **Escalation:** inside the cinema, the projection room is full of VCRs playing Tapes 1
  to 3. Pinned to the wall are addresses, each crossed out: Shanti Vihar, the toll plaza,
  Sagar Lodge.
- **Ending:** the last address on the wall isn't crossed out. It's Nikhil's address.
  Imran's phone buzzes: "Order delivered." The tape cuts to black.

## The Final Tape and Endings

Nikhil · his own room · tonight

A fifth tape sits in the box. Nikhil bought four. He plays it, and the TV shows his own
room tonight, filmed from the corner near the window. The narration starts, and it's
Nikhil's voice, calmly describing a night that hasn't finished yet.

**Gameplay:** Nikhil moves through his real room while the TV shows the recording. The two
stay in sync until the tape starts showing things a few seconds before they happen: a
knock, the light flickering, his phone ringing.

**The turn:** on the TV, the Cameraman is standing in the room behind Nikhil. In the real
room, the corner is empty, except for a small red dot.

### Ending A: Eject (main ending)

Nikhil rips the tape out, stuffs the box into a bag and dumps it in a bin far from home.
Cut to the following Sunday: the box is back on a flea-market stall. Someone picks it up.
Inside, there are now six tapes. The last frame is a close-up of the newest tape, warm
from the VCR.

### Ending B: Rewind (true ending)

Unlocked only by spotting the Cameraman in every tape (see Motifs). Nikhil picks up his
own old camcorder and turns it on the corner. On its viewfinder, the Cameraman is clearly
there, and for the first time he lowers his camera. There's no face behind it, just
another lens. The screen fills with static, and the box on the floor now holds only blank
tapes. The final shot leaves it ambiguous whether Nikhil broke the cycle or took the
Cameraman's place.

## Motifs and the Spotting Mechanic

**Spotting the Cameraman:** each tape hides three brief appearances of the Cameraman: in a
CCTV feed, a side mirror, a corridor's far end, a reflection in a dark window. The player
spots one by looking at it and pressing a key, which freezes the frame with a VHS pause
effect. Spotting all twelve unlocks Ending B. This gives replay value and teaches players
to scan every dark corner.

### Recurring motifs

- **The red dot.** Any red light (a standby LED, a tail light, a switchboard bulb) might be
  him. Use real red lights as decoys.
- **Tracking errors.** The VHS picture warps and rolls more as the Cameraman gets closer.
  It works as a proximity warning built into the visual style.
- **Paper records.** Every tape has a log the player writes in: a gate register, plate log,
  hotel ledger, order app. Each one eventually contains an entry the subject didn't write.
- **The time 3:12.** It appears in every tape and on Nikhil's diary entry.
- **Previous subjects.** From Tape 2 on, every tape brings back an earlier subject or
  place, tying the anthology together. In Tape 3, Salim's toll receipt sits in the lodge
  ledger.

## Notes for Development

- Build the hub and Tape 1 first, and release them together as a free demo.
- **Pacing:** follow the Fears to Fathom rhythm. Spend about 70% of each tape on normal
  tasks, 20% on growing wrongness, and 10% on the ending. Scares should be rare.
- **Reusable systems:** every tape uses the same interaction, objective, log-writing,
  trigger and spotting systems. Only the setting and the script change.
- **Assets per tape:** one main location, 15 to 30 props, one or two characters besides
  the subject.
- **Voice:** each tape needs about 2 to 4 minutes of narration. Record placeholder lines
  yourself first.
